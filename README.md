# The Real Truth of Ill Smith

Primal Code Map — Founding Text

## 01 ORIGIN

The name was created in 1986 by his brother, Sean Hampton. Born in Indianapolis, an army brat, he lived in Germany for eleven years. Then New Orleans, in the years Cash Money, Master P, and Mystikal were created. He was a child — an absorber only.

## 02 THE HINGE

The week he arrived in the Seattle area, Kurt Cobain died. That loss turned him into an artist. A kid watched a city lose its voice and decided his job was to keep speaking.

## 03 THE CRAFT

At sixteen, he watched Demrick, Casanova, and J.P. rhyme after school in a basement with other MCs. (Demrick now works with Cypress Hill.) He made his first album with a group called EWP — Earth, Wind & Pimpin' — with Wave, Joe Gotta, and himself. His name at the time: E. Pimp.

## 04 COLUMBIA

College years in Columbia, Missouri. He recorded live at underground clubs, including Down Under. In Seattle he was a studio artist; in Columbia, he was a live artist.

## 05 SEATTLE RETURN

He moved back to Seattle to be a studio artist for Microsoft. It never panned out. His group still included Joe Gotta and Bishop Blind, mentioned in Shine's music — the same Blind who died many years later.

## 06 INDIANA

He moved to Indiana, lived privately, and tried to record with no money. A house with no carpet: nothing but a computer, an Audio Surgeon, and an old version of Acid. That was the whole recording setup. First it was freestyles. The very first song out of all that is something obscure he never even wrote down — a raw, trashy freestyle period on bare-bones gear. He could probably pull it up in an old archive.

## 07 BOSTON

He moved to Boston, where his mother had moved, and lived with her about two years, acquiring equipment. His first beat machine: a Yamaha DX-200. Those beats fed more freestyles — not very good, not groundbreaking, never released. A Radio Shack microphone hung from the ceiling, recording into the microphone plug on an older-style computer. No interface yet. He used Sound Forge to clean it up. It sucked pretty bad.

## 08 FLORIDA

He moved to Florida after his mother had a brain aneurysm. There he got his first mixing board — a black digital board with a CD loader — plus a Roland mixing board. He used the DX-200, bought an MPC 500, and made beats on it to produce his first record: Ill Smith the Album. The first time the name Ill Smith carried a full body of work.

## 09 UPGRADES

Back in Boston, he recorded a series of four more albums while steadily upgrading equipment. A Roland MV-8800 — sampler, sequencer, and effects in one box. Then a Roland X6. In 2018 he bought his first condenser mic: the MXL Blizzard, the cheaper one with the blue light. He used it to make Negative 1000 Degrees, released December 24, 2020 — 15 tracks.

## 10 TODAY

Two synthesizers. He runs an MPC Renaissance. A full-body studio. Three sets of monitors. Higher-quality product, higher-quality professional sound. Not a hobby rig — a label.

## 11 THE PRIMAL LAYER

In February 2026, on a vacant wooded road at about 3 a.m., he encountered something he connects to the Nephilim. He could not see it directly, but he sensed strong predatory intent, saw high branches broken at 8–10+ feet, and felt it had emerged from inside the earth. Before it came empowering dreams of Minerva, queen of earth, who taught unconditional love. In the dreams she felt cold, and as if he was naive. The sequence matters: love first, then the thing that wanted to eat him. Grace precedes threat. Survival is the response to both.

## 12 THE THESIS

Pain is the foundation. Cold is the teacher. The transfer is the act: my soul transferred to the world.

That I really am who I say I am.
